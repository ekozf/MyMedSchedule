MyMedSchedule helps you remember your medicines. It works like the pill box you may already have: your day has four compartments, Morning, Midday, Evening and Night, and you get a reminder when it is time.

Private by design
• No account and no sign-up
• Everything stays on your phone, in an encrypted database
• No ads, no tracking, no analytics
• Works fully offline

A simple pill box
• Your whole day at a glance: Morning, Midday, Evening, Night
• One big button to mark a dose as taken
• Large, easy-to-read text in the Atkinson Hyperlegible typeface
• Light and dark mode

For the whole family
• Keep track for several people on one phone, such as a parent or a child
• Each person has their own medicines, reminders and journal

Schedules that fit real life
• Every day, on chosen weekdays, every few days, or only when needed
• Every few hours, on a set weekday of the month (like the 2nd Tuesday), or in cycles (like 21 days on, 7 days off)
• Tapering doses that step down over time
• Several times a day, each with its own amount

Reminders
• On-time reminders, also after your phone restarts
• Take, snooze for 15 minutes or skip, right from the notification

Supply and your own limits
• Count your supply and get a reminder when you are running low
• Keep track of expiry dates
• Optional: set your own daily limit and the minimum time between doses. If a dose goes over what you entered, the app shows a gentle warning. It never stops you, and it does not check whether a dose is medically right.

Journal and reports
• See what you took or skipped, day by day
• Forgot to tap? Log a past dose
• Make a PDF report to share with your doctor. The PDF is not encrypted, and you choose where it goes.

Lock and language
• Lock the app with a PIN, or with your fingerprint or face if your phone supports it
• Locks itself after one minute in the background
• English, Nederlands and Türkçe

Please note: MyMedSchedule is a reminder and a diary, not medical advice. It does not check your medicines for interactions. Always follow your doctor, your pharmacist and the leaflet.
